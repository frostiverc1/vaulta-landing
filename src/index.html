Why {{NAME}}

Every cloud notetaker is a witness you didn’t invite.

Sending a privileged conversation through a third-party AI service can put a vendor inside the communication loop. Storing the verbatim transcript creates a permanent record to be subpoenaed. {{NAME}} removes both risks by design.

Risk 01

Third-party privilege waiver

Routing client conversations through cloud LLM APIs invites opposing counsel to challenge privilege. With {{NAME}}, there is no third party. The model, the audio, and the memo never leave your Mac.

Risk 02

Discoverable verbatim transcripts

Tentative negotiating positions and off-the-cuff remarks shouldn’t live in a database forever. {{NAME}} keeps the transcript in volatile memory only, then overwrites it when you approve the memo.

How it works

Capture. Draft. Shred.

Three steps. One outcome: you keep the memo, and nothing else.

1

Capture, without a bot.

{{NAME}} taps the macOS audio system directly. No virtual drivers, no meeting bot joining the call, and no recurring screen-recording prompts.

2

Draft on the Neural Engine.

Whisper transcribes. A compact language model structures the deal memo. Client names are masked to your code-name dictionary as the text is written.

3

Approve and shred.

You review the memo. One click overwrites the verbatim transcript in memory and issues a signed receipt recording that no internet connection was observed and the session process exited.

Locked in physical RAM

Audio and transcripts are pinned with mlock and encrypted in memory, so the vault is never paged to disk. If the Mac sleeps, the vault is zeroed first.

Numbers that check out

Every currency figure, percentage, and date in the memo is matched against the transcript. Anything that can’t be verified is flagged in amber before you approve.

Code names, automatically

Deal Rooms carry a code-name dictionary, synced end-to-end encrypted. Client and counterparty names are masked in the memo from the first draft.

Sleep-safe

If the lid closes mid-review, a sleep hook zeroes the vault and ends the session.

Questions, answered.

Does {{NAME}} ever send audio or transcripts to a server?

No. Capture, transcription, and memo generation all happen on your Mac. The cloud control plane handles seats, policy, and signed receipts only, and never receives audio or transcript text.

Does a bot join my meetings?

No. {{NAME}} captures audio at the system level, so there’s nothing to admit to the call and nothing for other participants to see.

What happens to the transcript?

It lives in encrypted, pinned memory only. When you approve the memo, the buffer is overwritten. If the countdown expires first, your organization’s policy decides whether the draft is shredded or kept for later review.

Can I trust the numbers in the memo?

Every figure is checked against the transcript. Any number that can’t be matched is flagged [VERIFY FIGURE] so a human confirms it before approval.

Which Macs are supported?

Any Mac with Apple silicon (M1 or later) running macOS 14.2 or newer.
